## 1. Plain-English summary

At millimetre-wave frequencies, radios use many antennas and very wide bandwidths. Giving every antenna a precise analog-to-digital converter (ADC) would use too much power. So the authors ask a simple question: if each converter keeps only 1-4 bits, can the receiver still learn the radio channel? Here "the channel" means how the signal travels from every transmit antenna to every receive antenna, including echoes that arrive late.

Their answer is yes, provided the receiver uses two facts:
1. A millimetre-wave channel consists of a few strong paths. If you describe it on a grid of "arrival direction x departure direction x delay", almost every entry is close to zero, so the channel is sparse.
2. The receiver knows exactly how its coarse converter rounds numbers. It can therefore compute the probability that a candidate channel would have produced the rounded values it actually saw.

They combine these two facts with fast statistical algorithms called approximate message passing (GAMP and VAMP). These algorithms alternate between "what do the rounded measurements say?" and "what do sparse channels look like?". An expectation-maximisation (EM) step learns from the data itself how sparse the channel is, so nobody has to supply that number.

They also design a pilot (training) signal made of cyclically shifted Zadoff-Chu sequences. With this pilot every heavy step becomes a fast Fourier transform, so even 64x64-antenna links with 16 delay taps run quickly. The pilot also has a low peak-to-average power ratio, which is good for the transmitter's amplifier.

In simulations their EM-VAMP and EM-GAMP estimators beat least squares, linear MMSE, l1-norm sparse recovery (SPGL1) and quantised iterative hard thresholding (QIHT). With 1-bit converters the loss is small at low SNR, and 3-4-bit converters are almost as good as perfect ones up to medium SNR.

## 2. The problem and the system model

**Their setting (Sections II-IV, pp. 2-7).**
- **Hardware.** Fully digital MIMO: Nt transmit antennas and Nr receive antennas, arranged as uniform planar arrays (UPAs). Each receive antenna has two few-bit ADCs, one for the real part and one for the imaginary part (Fig. 1). Nothing is assumed about the transmitter's structure.
- **Channel.** Broadband, with delay spread L symbol taps: H[l], l = 0..L-1, each of size Nr x Nt (eq. 1). It is built from Ncl clusters of paths (eq. 4).
- **Angle-domain view.** Each tap is written on a grid of angles as H[l] = B_Nr X[l] B_Nt^* (eq. 5). For UPAs, B is a Kronecker product of unitary DFT matrices (eq. 6). Entry (i, j) of X[l] is the gain between the j-th grid departure angle and the i-th grid arrival angle.
- **Leakage.** X is only approximately sparse, because paths that fall between grid points spread their energy over neighbouring entries (Sec. III-B, p. 4).
- **Training.** One block T of size Nt x Np, sent with a cyclic prefix of length L. After the prefix is removed, Y = Q(Z + W), where Z = sum_l H[l] T J_l and J_l is the l-circulant delay matrix (eqs. 7-11).
- **Vector form.** y = Q(A x + w) with A = C^T kron B_Nr (eqs. 12-13). This is "noisy quantised compressed sensing": find the sparse x from the rounded vector y.
- **Quantiser.** Uniform mid-rise, applied to the real and imaginary parts separately (eq. 2). The step equals RMS x Delta_b, where the RMS comes from automatic gain control (AGC) and Delta_b is the mean-square-error-optimal step for a unit-variance Gaussian input (Table I, from Max 1960). The outermost cells are open-ended, so large values saturate.

## 3. The method, step by step

### 3.1 The big picture in four steps
1. **Turn the problem into a sparse one.** Write the channel on a DFT angle grid times the delay taps, so that most unknowns are close to zero (eqs. 5-6).
2. **Write down the exact rounding likelihood.** A measurement y_i is explained by z_i (the noiseless value) if z_i plus noise falls inside the ADC cell that produced y_i (eqs. 19-20).
3. **Run approximate message passing with a learned prior.** GAMP (Algorithm 1) or VAMP (Algorithm 2) alternates two cheap per-entry "denoisers" with multiplications by A. After every iteration an EM step re-fits the sparse prior. The prior is Bernoulli-Gaussian (BG) or a Bernoulli Gaussian mixture (GM) (eqs. 17-18).
4. **Make it fast.** Choose shifted Zadoff-Chu pilots. Then every A x and A^* s is a set of FFTs (eqs. 21-28), and A has all-equal singular values (eqs. 29-31). That is ideal for GAMP's convergence and makes VAMP's linear step trivial.

### 3.2 The key equations, one sentence each
- **Eq. (2), quantiser.** Each real or imaginary value is replaced by the centre of its uniform cell; the cell width is the RMS level times a tabulated step Delta_b, and values beyond the last cell saturate.
- **Eq. (5) / (6), angle domain.** The antenna-domain channel equals a DFT, times a sparse angle-domain matrix X, times a DFT.
- **Eq. (13), measurement.** y = Q(A x + w), with A = C^T kron B_Nr: the noisy, rounded version of a known linear transform of the sparse vector x.
- **Eqs. (17)-(18), prior.** Each grid coefficient is exactly zero with probability lambda_0; otherwise it is complex Gaussian (BG), or drawn from a mixture of complex Gaussians (GM).
- **Eqs. (19)-(20), likelihood.** p(y_i | z_i) is the probability that Gaussian noise moves z_i into the observed cell. This is the "exact quantised likelihood", and it has the same form as our interval-censored Gaussian likelihood in mlq.py.
- **Eqs. (14)-(15), scalar posteriors.** The two "denoisers" are ordinary one-variable Bayes posteriors: z_i given a Gaussian pseudo-prior and the cell likelihood, and x_i given a Gaussian pseudo-measurement and the sparse prior.
- **Eq. (30) / (31), training design.** With a Zadoff-Chu sequence, T~ T~^* is a multiple of the identity, so every singular value of A equals sqrt(Pt Np / Nt).
- **Eqs. (34)-(39), Bussgang linearisation.** These are used only by the benchmarks and the rate analysis. The quantiser output is treated as (1 - eta_b) x signal + uncorrelated distortion, and the effective noise variance is (1 - eta_b)(sigma_w^2 + eta_b Pt L sigma_x^2).
- **Eqs. (44)-(46), norm estimation.** A 1-bit output has lost all amplitude. So every estimate is rescaled to the channel norm, which is predicted from the pre-ADC received power that AGC measures (this requires knowing sigma_w^2).

### 3.3 EM-GAMP (Algorithm 1, p. 5), in words
Initialise s = 0, the prior parameters theta, x at the prior mean and nu_x at the prior variance. Then, at every iteration:
1. **Output side** (lines 4-9). Predict each measurement's noiseless value p = A x - nu_p s, with variance nu_p = \|\|A\|\|_F^2 nu_x / Ny. Compute the posterior mean and variance of each z_i under the cell likelihood and the pseudo-prior CN(p_i, nu_p). Turn these into a residual s and its variance nu_s.
2. **Input side** (lines 10-13). Form the pseudo-measurement r = x + nu_r A^* s, with 1/nu_r = \|\|A\|\|_F^2 nu_s / Nx. Apply the sparse-prior denoiser to each entry to get the new x and nu_x.
3. **EM step** (line 14). Re-fit theta (the sparsity rate and the Gaussian weights, means and variances).

GAMP's theory (state evolution) only holds for large i.i.d. sub-Gaussian A. The paper says this guarantee does not hold for its structured A, but that fewer than 25 iterations are usually close to MMSE (Sec. IV-B, p. 5).

### 3.4 EM-VAMP (Algorithm 2, p. 6), in words
VAMP keeps two copies of every estimate and passes "extrinsic" messages between them:
1. **Denoise x** (lines 5-7) under the sparse prior, starting from r1 with variance nu1. The average posterior variance divided by nu1 gives alpha1. The extrinsic message is r2 = (x1 - alpha1 r1)/(1 - alpha1), with nu2 = nu1 alpha1/(1 - alpha1).
2. **Denoise z** (lines 9-11) under the cell likelihood, starting from p1 with variance tau1. This gives beta1, then p2 and tau2 in the same way.
3. **LMMSE for x** (lines 13-15). Combine the pseudo-prior x ~ CN(r2, nu2) with the pseudo-measurement p2 = A x + CN(0, tau2) to get x2 and alpha2, then send back r1 and nu1.
4. **LMMSE for z** (lines 17-19). Set z2 = A x2 and compute beta2, then send back p1 and tau1.
5. **EM step** (line 20).

VAMP's state evolution holds for "right-rotationally invariant" A, a much wider class than GAMP's (p. 5). In Fig. 6 VAMP converges in about 3 iterations, GAMP in about 8 and QIHT in about 35 (p. 10).

**Printing issue to watch (Algorithm 2, line 18).** The paper prints beta2 = (1 - alpha2) Ny/Nx. If beta2 is defined, like beta1 and alpha2, as the average posterior variance divided by the prior variance, it must be (1 - alpha2) **Nx/Ny**.
- I checked this numerically with random complex A of sizes 256x256, 256x1024 and 512x256 (`mo2018_vamp_beta2_check.log`). beta2 matches (1 - alpha2) Nx/Ny exactly every time. It matches Ny/Nx only when Ny = Nx.
- The GLM-VAMP paper (arXiv 1612.01186, eq. 19, p. 4) prints the same inverted ratio M/N. Its middle expression, (1/M) sum_n tau s_n^2/(tau s_n^2 + gamma), equals (N/M)(1 - alpha2).
- This makes no difference in the paper's Np = 1024 case (Ny = Nx) or in our on-grid G = 1 case. It does matter for an oversampled grid (Nx > Ny). **Use Nx/Ny.**
- Whether the authors' MATLAB code (gampmatlab) uses the correct ratio is UNVERIFIED; I did not inspect the code.

### 3.5 The two scalar denoisers
The paper does not print these formulas. It refers to its refs. [27] and [43, Ch. 3.9] and to [6, App. A]. The forms below are standard Gaussian algebra. The EM updates are verified in Vila and Schniter (arXiv 1207.3107), eqs. (34), (41), (47) on pp. 4-5. The truncated-normal moments I derived myself, and they are consistent with mlq.nll_terms (see the note below).

**Input denoiser for a zero-mean complex BG prior.** Use lambda = P(x != 0) = 1 - lambda_0. Given r = x + CN(0, nu):
- pi = 1 / (1 + ((1 - lambda)/lambda) x CN(r; 0, nu) / CN(r; 0, phi + nu)). This is the posterior probability that the entry is "on".
- gamma = r phi/(phi + nu) and v = phi nu/(phi + nu): the posterior mean and variance if the entry is on.
- Posterior mean: x_hat = pi gamma. Posterior variance: pi (\|gamma\|^2 + v) - \|x_hat\|^2.
- EM updates: lambda_new = mean(pi) and phi_new = sum pi (\|gamma\|^2 + v) / sum pi. These are Vila and Schniter (34) and (47) with the mean fixed at 0, as in Mo et al. eq. (18).

**Output denoiser for one ADC cell.** Take one real component with pseudo-prior N(p, v), where v = tau/2. The noise per real component is N(0, sigma_w^2/2), and we know y + noise lies in [lo, hi]. Set
- s = sqrt(v + sigma_w^2/2), a = (lo - p)/s, b = (hi - p)/s, Zc = Phi(b) - Phi(a).
- Posterior mean: E[z] = p + (v/s)(phi(a) - phi(b))/Zc.
- Posterior variance: Var[z] = v + (v^2/s^2) [ (a phi(a) - b phi(b))/Zc - ((phi(a) - phi(b))/Zc)^2 ].

**Reuse note.** These are exactly E[z] = p - v g and Var[z] = v - v^2 h, where (g, h) are the first and second derivatives of the cell negative log-likelihood. `finetune/adversarial/mlq.py: nll_terms(mu=p, lo, hi, s)` already returns these derivatives in a numerically safe form. The complex posterior variance is the sum of the real-part and imaginary-part variances.

### 3.6 Training design and fast implementation (Sec. IV-D, pp. 6-7)
- **Three goals:** A must be fast to apply; A must have a low peak-to-average ratio of squared singular values (for GAMP to converge); T must have a low PAPR (for the power amplifier).
- **Design:** transmit antenna n sends the first row t of T, circularly shifted by nL places (eq. 21). Np must be an integer multiple of Nt L.
- **Fast products** (eqs. 22-28): with a commutation matrix, A x and A^* s reduce to Nr L FFTs of length Np, one inverse FFT of length Np, and a few small 2D FFTs. A never has to be stored.
- **Why it matters:** with Nt = Nr = 64, Np = 1024 and L = 16, an explicit A would take 32 GB (p. 6).
- **Choice of t:** a Zadoff-Chu sequence (eq. 31). It gives equal singular values, which is best for GAMP, and constant modulus, which is low PAPR. V_A = I and U_A = sqrt(Nt/(Pt Np)) A, so VAMP is also fast (p. 7).
- **Alternatives tried:** Golay, i.i.d. QPSK and i.i.d. Gaussian training give similar NMSE but lack these fast operators (Fig. 4, p. 9).

### 3.7 Benchmarks and the norm fix (Secs. IV-E and IV-F, pp. 7-8)
- **LS** (eq. 33).
- **ALMMSE**: the linear MMSE estimator on the Bussgang-linearised model (eq. 40).
- **SPGL1**: basis pursuit denoising on the linearised model (eq. 41).
- **QIHT** (eqs. 42-43), with x(1) = 0, tau = 0.1 Nt/(Pt Np) and K = Nx/100, i.e. the largest 1% of entries are kept.
- They also tried an l1 method (their ref. [37]) but had numerical problems, and CVX could not hold A in memory (footnote 4, p. 8).
- Every estimator's output is rescaled by the norm estimate of eq. (46) (p. 8).

### 3.8 Re-implementation pseudo-code: "EMVAMP-Q", the paper's EM-BG-VAMP adapted to our narrowband 16x16 beamspace
Anything not in the paper is marked [OURS]. Tune only on validation seeds 81,000,000+.

```
INPUT  Yq (16x16 complex, output of stress_sim.adc), bits b, sigma2 = 10^(-SNR/10) (assumed known, as in the paper and in our ML),
       grid oversampling G in {1, 2, 4, 8} [OURS], L = 3 for peak picking only [OURS; the paper needs no L], K_max = 30
CELLS  (lo, hi) for the 512 real components = acommon.adc_cells(Yq, b) (peak AGC: the receiver knows its own cells)
GRID   g_i = 2*pi*i/(16G), i = 0..16G-1;  R = W^H exp(-j n g^T) (16 x 16G);  T = F^T exp(+j n g^T) (16 x 16G)
       A x := vec(R X T^T)/16,  A^H y := vec(R^H Ymat conj(T))/16      (X is 16G x 16G, column-major vec)
       c := G^2, because A A^H = c I. Checked in mo2018_grid_A_check.log: G = 1 gives A unitary with ONE nonzero per column;
       G = 2, 4 give all 256 singular values equal to G.
       Ny = 256, Nx = 256 G^2
INIT   [OURS] lambda = 0.05 (G = 1) or 0.01 (G >= 2);  P = mean(|Yq|^2) - sigma2 (floored at 1e-3);
       phi = P*Ny/(c*lambda*Nx);  r1 = 0, nu1 = lambda*phi;  p1 = 0, tau1 = P
REPEAT k = 1..K_max
  # (a) input denoiser, BG prior (3.5)
  pi, gamma, v   <- BG posterior of x given r1, nu1
  x1  = pi*gamma ;  var1 = pi*(|gamma|^2 + v) - |x1|^2 ;  alpha1 = mean(var1)/nu1
  r2  = (x1 - alpha1*r1)/(1 - alpha1) ;  nu2 = nu1*alpha1/(1 - alpha1)
  # (b) output denoiser, exact ADC cells (3.5), done separately for Re and Im
  s   = sqrt((tau1 + sigma2)/2) ;  (_, g, h) = mlq.nll_terms(mu=[Re p1, Im p1], lo, hi, s)
  z1  = p1 - (tau1/2)*g  (as complex) ;  varz = sum over Re/Im of (tau1/2 - (tau1/2)^2*h) ;  beta1 = mean(varz)/tau1
  p2  = (z1 - beta1*p1)/(1 - beta1) ;  tau2 = tau1*beta1/(1 - beta1)
  # (c) LMMSE for x, closed form because A A^H = c I (Alg. 2, lines 13-15)
  x2  = r2 + nu2/(c*nu2 + tau2) * A^H (p2 - A r2)
  alpha2 = (Ny/Nx)*tau2/(c*nu2 + tau2) + (1 - Ny/Nx)
  r1  = (x2 - alpha2*r2)/(1 - alpha2) ;  nu1 = nu2*alpha2/(1 - alpha2)
  # (d) LMMSE for z (Alg. 2, lines 17-19), with beta2 using Nx/Ny (see 3.4)
  z2  = A x2 ;  beta2 = (1 - alpha2)*Nx/Ny
  p1  = (z2 - beta2*p2)/(1 - beta2) ;  tau1 = tau2*beta2/(1 - beta2)
  # (e) EM (Vila & Schniter eqs. 34, 47; zero mean)
  lambda = mean(pi) ;  phi = sum(pi*(|gamma|^2 + v))/sum(pi)
  # (f) [OURS] damping 0.5 on (r1, nu1, p1, tau1); clip alpha, beta to [1e-6, 1 - 1e-6]; stop if ||dx1||/||x1|| < 1e-4
RETURN x1 (grid MMSE estimate) and pi (posterior support map, 16G x 16G)
PEAKS  [OURS] M = |x1| on the wrap-around 16G x 16G grid; take the L largest 3x3 local maxima (wrap-around); map (i, j) to (u, v),
       then to angles with the benchmark's convention (reuse ucore.uv_to_deg)
REFINE [OURS, optional variants] (R1) parabolic interpolation of |x1| around each peak;
       (R2) quantiser-ML Levenberg-Marquardt from these L seeds (mlq.lm / mlt.lm_t), i.e. "EM-VAMP-seeded quantiser ML"
```

Note on (c) and (d): with G = 1 we have c = 1 and Ny = Nx, so step (c) reduces to Algorithm 2's line 13 with S_A = I.

## 6. Citable claims (paraphrased; location verified on arXiv v4)

1. **Main conclusion (Abstract, p. 1).** Exploiting joint angle-delay sparsity lets 1-bit ADCs "perform comparably to infinite-bit ADCs at low SNR", and lets 4-bit ADCs do so at medium SNR. *Use:* motivation that few-bit ADCs are viable for mmWave channel estimation when the estimator is quantisation-aware. Say clearly that this is NMSE for a fully digital broadband array, not 1-deg angle accuracy.
2. **First noisy-quantised-CS formulation (Sec. I, contributions, p. 2).** To the authors' knowledge, they were the first to cast broadband mmWave channel estimation with few-bit ADCs as noisy quantised compressed sensing using angle and delay sparsity. *Use:* credit for the sparse-grid, quantisation-aware formulation, and the reason we must not claim it.
3. **Exact quantised likelihood inside AMP (Sec. IV-C, eqs. 19-20, p. 6).** The likelihood is the probability that Gaussian noise moves z_i into the observed quantiser cell. *Use:* credit when we describe our quantiser-ML likelihood; it has the same form, so it is not our novelty.
4. **Few-bit loss depends on SNR (Sec. V-C, Fig. 8, p. 10).** With EM-VAMP, the 1-bit vs infinite-bit NMSE gap is only about 2 dB below 0 dB SNR and grows as SNR rises; higher resolution helps only at higher SNR. *Use:* explains why our 1-bit cells hurt most at 15 dB.
5. **Little gain beyond 4 bits (Sec. II, p. 3; Sec. V-C, Fig. 9, p. 11).** At SNR 10 dB, EM-GAMP/VAMP gain little from more than 4 bits; the paper also predicts this from the SQNR rule (about 5b dB). *Use:* justifies studying 1-4 bits and a bit-aware switch.
6. **Quantisation-aware estimators beat linearised ones (Sec. V-C, Fig. 9, p. 11).** SPGL1, which ignores the structure of the quantisation error, matches EM-BG-GAMP/VAMP only above 7 bits; LS and ALMMSE are far from optimal (Sec. V-B, p. 10). *Use:* supports using exact-likelihood decoders rather than treating quantisation as Gaussian noise.
7. **Speed (Sec. V-B, Figs. 6-7, p. 10; Table II, p. 11).** VAMP converges in about 3 iterations and GAMP in about 8. EM-GM-VAMP has the best NMSE-complexity trade-off for runtimes above 0.4 s (MATLAB). All AMP methods scale as N log N with FFT operators. *Use:* when comparing latency. Their runtimes are MATLAB on a 64x64 broadband problem, so they are not comparable to our ms/scene.
8. **Stated limitation (footnote 1, p. 6; Sec. VI, p. 13).** The prior is i.i.d.; correlation among angle-delay coefficients is ignored, and exploiting it is left as future work. *Use:* a structural prior (exactly L continuous paths, or a learned heatmap) is a legitimate direction that the paper itself names.

## 7. Limitations and what the paper does not show
- **Metric.** It reports only NMSE, mutual information and rate. There is no angle, delay or path-parameter accuracy, so nothing about 1-deg detection.
- **On-grid model.** Off-grid paths become leakage, and there is no super-resolution step. Our `mo2018_grid_error.log` shows the cost for our angle metric: snapping noise-free angles to a 16-point DFT grid puts only 17.8% of angle components within 1 deg (median error 2.8 deg). With 4x oversampling the figure is 68.8%, and with 8x it is 85.5%.
- **Architecture.** Fully digital with 2 Nr ADCs and long digital training (Np = 1024-5120). There is no analog or hybrid beamforming, so the beam-sweep regime (16x16 = 256 measurements) is not covered.
- **Known noise variance; idealised AGC.** The noise variance is assumed known; AGC measures the RMS perfectly and uses the Max-optimal step. Mismatch is never tested: no wrong noise level, no peak AGC, no clipping study.
- **Statistical reporting.** Only 100 channel realisations, with no confidence intervals or significance tests. Runtimes are for object-oriented MATLAB, and the paper itself says the first VAMP iteration is slow for that reason (p. 10).
- **Theory does not strictly apply.** GAMP and VAMP guarantees need random A (i.i.d. or right-rotationally invariant). The paper admits this for GAMP (p. 5). It does not discuss that its ZC design gives V_A = I, which is not Haar-random, so VAMP's guarantee does not strictly hold either (my observation).
- **No comparison** with gridless or maximum-likelihood parametric estimators, or with learned methods.
- **Printed issues.** Algorithm 2 line 18 has an inverted Ny/Nx ratio (section 3.4), and the text says "exponential" in some places and "polynomial" in another (section 5).
- **Single-user, synchronised, static channel.** No phase noise, CFO or other hardware impairments besides the ADC.

## 8. Relevance to us

**What to reuse.**
- **The output channel.** Their likelihood (eqs. 19-20) is our interval-censored Gaussian. mlq.nll_terms / mlt.nll_q already compute it, along with the moments a GAMP/VAMP output denoiser needs (section 3.5). Credit: "exact quantiser likelihood as in GAMP-based few-bit estimation [Mo et al. 2018]".
- **A training-free sparse prior with EM** (BG, or GM with about 3 components). This gives a classical posterior support map pi over an angle grid without any training data.
- **The structure of our A** (my check, `mo2018_grid_A_check.log`). With our DFT codebooks, the measurement matrix on the 16x16 grid is unitary with exactly **one nonzero per column**.
  - So **on-grid EM-VAMP decouples into 256 independent per-beam scalar problems**: no compressed-sensing coupling at all.
  - With oversampling (G = 2, 4), all 256 nonzero singular values equal G (a tight frame). The paper's "equal singular values" design goal (Sec. IV-D) therefore holds automatically for us, and the VAMP LMMSE step is closed form.
- **Not needed:** the norm fix (Sec. IV-F) does not matter for Pd, because angles do not depend on scale.

**Must it be a baseline? Yes.** It is the standard published few-bit mmWave estimator built on an exact quantised likelihood, and reviewers in this area will expect it (our unfolding survey, row [32], lists it). It has to be adapted to angle estimation, and the adaptation must be described honestly as "EM-BG-VAMP of Mo et al. (2018) with peak extraction". Pre-register these variants:
- **V0, faithful:** G = 1 DFT grid, L largest peaks. Its noise-free ceiling is about 0.18 of angle components within 1 deg, so we report it for faithfulness, not as a competitor.
- **V1, oversampled:** G = 4 (ceiling 0.69) and G = 8 (0.85). Risk: AMP-type algorithms can become unstable with a coherent (oversampled) dictionary. Use damping and report how often it diverges.
- **V2:** V1 plus quantiser-ML refinement from the L EM-VAMP peaks (one start). This is the direct classical rival to our hybrid (CNN seeds plus quantiser ML).
- **Optionally** EM-GM, which the paper finds slightly better at high SNR.

**Fair-comparison rules.**
- Same test banks (seeds 12000-12999, 1000 scenes per cell) and the same cells: 1 and 2 bits at 0 and 15 dB (add 3 and 4 bits if time allows).
- Same knowledge for every method: known sigma2 (as for quantiser ML), known L = 3 for peak picking (state that the paper does not need L), and the same peak-AGC cells from acommon.adc_cells.
- Same metrics: paper-style Pd, strict Pd and RMSE after Hungarian matching, with paired bootstrap (2000 resamples within SNR groups, seed 0).
- Tune damping, G, initialisation and the iteration count only on validation seeds 81,000,000+.
- Measure latency on 1 thread, as in s6_latency.json.
- Optional AGC sensitivity check with stress_sim.adc(agc='rms3'). Note that this is not identical to the paper's Table I step either.

**Current numbers this baseline must face** (RESULTS_SUMMARY section 6, fresh seeds, Pd):
- 1 bit, 15 dB: quantiser ML 0.540; hybrid 0.572 (+0.032*); ML from the true angles 0.626.
- 2 bits, 15 dB: quantiser ML 0.682; hybrid 0.687 (tie).
- At 0 dB, ML and the hybrid tie.
- **EM-VAMP results do not exist yet. Nothing about them may be claimed until the runs above are logged.**

**What this means for the network design.**
1. The exact quantised likelihood is prior art (this paper, building on GAMP). Our novelty has to be the seeding, speed or robustness, never the likelihood.
2. EM-VAMP's posterior support map is the classical, training-free counterpart of our CNN heatmap. To claim that the network is needed for seeding, CNN-seeded quantiser ML must beat EM-VAMP-seeded quantiser ML with a confidence interval that excludes 0. If they tie, the +0.032 at 1 bit / 15 dB is not evidence for learning.
3. If we unfold, "unfolded from EM-VAMP" is a known route (LVAMP, Borgerding et al. 2017). Our survey notes that LVAMP gains nothing over a matched VAMP for i.i.d. signals. Our signal is three off-grid paths, not i.i.d. on a grid, so a structured 2D denoiser might add value, but that is only a hypothesis.
4. EM-VAMP needs sigma2, just as ML does. The CNN read-out does not. Keep that advantage visible, since ML loses 0.04-0.07 Pd under a 5 dB mismatch.
5. One EM-VAMP iteration costs a few 16 x 16G matrix products. It may well be faster than the CNN (6.2 ms), so latency tables must include it once measured.

## 9. "If a reviewer asks..."

**Q1. Mo et al. already estimate few-bit mmWave channels with EM-VAMP and an exact quantised likelihood. What is new here?**
- They estimate the whole broadband channel on a DFT grid, measured by NMSE, with a fully digital 64x64 array and 1024-5120 training symbols.
- We estimate continuous AoA/AoD to within 1 deg from a single 16x16 analog beam sweep (256 scalar measurements), with peak AGC.
- We do not claim the quantised likelihood. We credit it to them and to the GAMP line of work.
- Our claim, if it survives, is narrower: network-found starting points plus quantiser ML beat multi-start quantiser ML at 1 bit / 15 dB (+0.032, CI excludes 0, replicated), tie elsewhere, and cost about 1/5 as much.
- Their algorithm, adapted to our setting, is reported as a baseline (section 8). Until that run exists, we cannot say how we compare to it.

**Q2. Why not use EM-VAMP's posterior as seeds instead of training a CNN?**
- We do not know yet. It is the decisive control.
- On a 16-point grid it cannot work alone: about 18% of angle components fall within 1 deg even without noise (`mo2018_grid_error.log`).
- With oversampling plus quantiser-ML refinement it may well match the CNN seeds.
- We will run "EM-VAMP seeds + ML" against "CNN seeds + ML" on the same banks. If they tie, we will report that the learned seeds add nothing over a training-free Bayesian seed, and keep only the speed and noise-level-free arguments.

**Q3. Their Fig. 8 shows 1-bit ADCs costing only about 2 dB. Why do your 1-bit results collapse (in the stress test at 15 dB, NOMP loses 0.75 Pd at 1 bit vs clean hardware on the same scenes; RESULTS_SUMMARY section 2)?**
- There is no contradiction. Their 2 dB is at SNR below 0 dB, and their own Fig. 8 shows the 1-bit gap widening to roughly 10 dB NMSE by 15 dB SNR (my plot reading). Our 15 dB cells sit exactly in that saturated regime.
- Pd at 1 deg is a much stricter parameter-level metric than an NMSE of about -12 dB.
- We have one beam sweep (Ny = Nx = 256 on the native grid), while they average over 1024-5120 training symbols (Ny/Nx from 1 to 5).
- Even plain NOMP under peak AGC is not quantisation-aware. With the quantisation-aware ML, 1-bit / 15 dB reaches 0.540 (0.572 hybrid).

## 10. Glossary (plain English)
- **ADC (analog-to-digital converter):** turns a voltage into a number; "b bits" means 2^b possible output levels.
- **Mid-rise uniform quantiser:** equally wide cells; outputs sit at cell centres, so zero is a cell boundary and not an output.
- **AGC (automatic gain control):** scales the signal before the ADC so it fits the ADC range. In the paper it is based on RMS; ours is based on the peak.
- **SQNR:** signal power divided by quantisation-error power. About 5 dB per bit (Table I).
- **NMSE:** squared error of the channel estimate divided by the channel's energy. Lower is better, usually given in dB.
- **Angle (beamspace) domain:** the channel described as gains between discrete departure and arrival directions, obtained with DFT matrices.
- **Angle-delay sparsity:** only a few direction-delay cells carry energy.
- **Leakage:** a path between grid points spreads energy into neighbouring cells, so the channel is only approximately sparse.
- **Compressed sensing:** recovering a sparse vector from fewer, or noisy, linear measurements. "Quantised" CS means the measurements are also rounded.
- **MMSE / LMMSE:** the estimate with the smallest average squared error. LMMSE is the best estimate among linear ones.
- **AMP / GAMP / VAMP:** fast iterative Bayesian algorithms that break a big estimation problem into scalar "denoising" steps plus matrix multiplications. GAMP allows any per-measurement likelihood, such as quantisation. VAMP works for a broader class of matrices and converges in fewer iterations.
- **State evolution:** a one-number recursion that predicts AMP's error exactly for large random matrices. That guarantee does not strictly hold for structured matrices.
- **Right-rotationally invariant (RRI):** a matrix whose right singular vectors are uniformly random. This is VAMP's theoretical condition.
- **Onsager / extrinsic correction:** the "- alpha r" and "/(1 - alpha)" terms. They remove the part of a message that the receiving step already knows, which keeps the errors Gaussian-like.
- **Bernoulli-Gaussian (BG) / Gaussian-mixture (GM) prior:** "each coefficient is zero with probability lambda_0, otherwise Gaussian (or a mixture of Gaussians)".
- **EM (expectation-maximisation):** alternately estimate the hidden values and re-fit the model parameters. Here it learns the sparsity rate and the variances from the measurements.
- **Bussgang decomposition:** writes a nonlinear device's output as a scaled input plus distortion uncorrelated with the input. The benchmarks and the rate bounds use it.
- **ALMMSE:** LMMSE on the Bussgang-linearised model, with an approximate noise covariance.
- **SPGL1 / BPDN:** a solver for "smallest l1 norm such that the residual is below a noise level", a classic convex sparse recovery.
- **QIHT:** quantised iterative hard thresholding; keep the K largest entries at every iteration.
- **Zadoff-Chu (ZC) sequence:** a constant-amplitude complex sequence whose cyclic shifts are orthogonal (as in LTE). Here it gives fast FFT products, equal singular values and low PAPR.
- **PAPR:** peak-to-average power ratio. Low PAPR lets the power amplifier work efficiently.
- **Cyclic prefix / circulant matrix:** repeating the end of the block at its start turns linear convolution with the channel into circular convolution, which FFTs diagonalise.
- **Kronecker product (kron) / commutation matrix K:** the algebra used to write the 2D (antenna x time) model as one matrix-vector product and to reorder it for fast FFTs.
- **UPA / ULA:** uniform planar (2D grid) or linear (1D) antenna array.
- **Coherence time Nco:** how many symbols the channel stays fixed. Training uses up part of it, which is the "training overhead".
- **Mutual information lower bound / achievable rate:** how many bits per channel use a link can carry with the estimated channel, treating estimation error as worst-case Gaussian noise.
- **OFDM / waterfilling:** a multicarrier transmission scheme, and the power allocation over its eigen-channels used in the rate analysis.
